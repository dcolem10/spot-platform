## 1. Washington, DC (Target: 75 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Shaw/U Street**: Trendy, chef-driven, cocktail bars. The beating heart of DC's modern dining scene
- **Capitol Hill / Eastern Market**: Neighborhood favorites, diverse cuisines, family-friendly
- **Adams Morgan**: International, late-night, diverse immigrant-owned restaurants
- **Georgetown**: Upscale, waterfront dining, historic charm
- **Penn Quarter / Chinatown**: Pre-theater, power lunch, upscale casual
- **Dupont Circle**: Mediterranean, brunch culture, sidewalk cafes
- **Logan Circle / 14th St**: New American, trendy cocktail-forward spots
- **Columbia Heights**: Diverse, affordable, immigrant cuisines (Ethiopian, Salvadoran, Vietnamese)
- **Navy Yard / The Wharf**: Waterfront, seafood, new development dining
- **Foggy Bottom**: Near Georgetown, international, GWU-adjacent
- **H Street NE (Atlas District)**: Up-and-coming, eclectic, creative concepts
- **Petworth / Brightwood**: Neighborhood gems, diverse cuisines

### Cuisine Strengths
DC is uniquely strong in: Ethiopian (largest Ethiopian population outside Africa), Salvadoran, Vietnamese, New American, Indian, and political-power fine dining. Also strong in: Korean, Japanese, Mediterranean, French bistro.

### Local Notes
- DC's dining scene punches above its weight nationally. Include Michelin-starred and James Beard-awarded spots
- Heavy brunch culture — many restaurants known specifically for weekend brunch
- Food halls are big (Union Market, The Wharf, La Cosecha)
- Government crowd drives weekday lunch business

## 2. Baltimore, MD (Target: 50 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Fells Point**: Waterfront, seafood, bars and restaurants
- **Federal Hill**: Young professional, sports bars, brunch spots
- **Harbor East**: Upscale, hotel dining, waterfront
- **Mount Vernon**: Arts district, diverse dining, cocktail bars
- **Hampden**: Quirky, independent, hipster-foodie
- **Canton**: Neighborhood dining, diverse options
- **Station North**: Arts district, up-and-coming
- **Remington**: Trendy, new restaurants, R. House food hall

### Cuisine Strengths
Baltimore is known for: Crab (obviously), seafood, Italian-American, Greek, Korean (Ellicott City corridor), soul food, and an emerging chef-driven scene. Good representation of: American diner/comfort food, pizza, Caribbean.

### Local Notes
- Crab houses are iconic but include modern interpretations
- Baltimore is more affordable than DC — price levels skew 1-2
- Strong neighborhood identity in dining choices

## 3. Arlington, VA (Target: 50 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Clarendon**: Dense restaurant corridor, young professional, diverse
- **Ballston**: Growing dining scene, chains mixed with independents
- **Crystal City / Pentagon City**: Business lunch, diverse fast-casual and sit-down
- **Rosslyn**: Near Georgetown bridge, business dining
- **Shirlington**: Walkable village, independent restaurants
- **Columbia Pike**: Diverse immigrant cuisines, affordable
- **Lee Highway Corridor**: Vietnamese, Korean, diverse Asian

### Cuisine Strengths
Arlington's diversity shines in: Vietnamese (Eden Center adjacent), Korean, Peruvian, Afghan, Ethiopian, Thai. Also strong in: American gastropub, pizza, ramen, Mediterranean.

### Local Notes
- Arlington benefits from DC spillover — many DC chefs open Arlington concepts
- Eden Center (technically Falls Church but close) is the Vietnamese food capital of the East Coast
- More suburban feel but walkable pockets around Metro stations

## 4. Alexandria, VA (Target: 50 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Old Town**: Historic waterfront, charming cobblestone streets, upscale casual
- **Del Ray**: "The small town in the big city" — independent, family-friendly, local favorites
- **Carlyle / Eisenhower**: Newer development, modern dining
- **West End**: Growing dining scene
- **Potomac Yard**: New development area

### Cuisine Strengths
Old Town Alexandria has: French, Italian, American fine dining, seafood, Southern. Del Ray is strong in: Brunch, pizza, Thai, American casual. The city overall has good: Mediterranean, Indian, Ethiopian representation.

### Local Notes
- Old Town has destination dining — people travel from DC for it
- Restaurant Row on King Street is a major draw
- More "charming" and "date night" vibe than DC's trendy scene
- Strong waterfront dining along the Potomac

## 5. New York, NY (Target: 100 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Manhattan — Lower East Side/East Village**: Trendy, chef-driven, cocktail bars, diverse
- **Manhattan — West Village/Greenwich Village**: Classic, romantic, Italian, French
- **Manhattan — SoHo/NoHo**: Upscale, fashion-forward dining
- **Manhattan — Chelsea/Flatiron**: Korean corridor, market dining (Chelsea Market)
- **Manhattan — Midtown**: Power dining, steakhouses, tourist-friendly
- **Manhattan — Chinatown/Little Italy**: Authentic Chinese, Vietnamese, classic Italian-American
- **Manhattan — Harlem**: Soul food, African, Caribbean, classic American
- **Manhattan — Upper West/East Side**: Neighborhood dining, classic NYC restaurants
- **Brooklyn — Williamsburg**: Hipster-foodie, farm-to-table, cocktail-forward
- **Brooklyn — DUMBO/Brooklyn Heights**: Waterfront, upscale casual
- **Brooklyn — Park Slope/Prospect Heights**: Family-friendly, diverse, Smorgasburg
- **Brooklyn — Bushwick**: Up-and-coming, creative, affordable
- **Queens — Flushing**: Best Chinese food in the US, Korean, Dim Sum
- **Queens — Jackson Heights**: Indian, Nepali, Colombian, Ecuadorian — most diverse food in the world
- **Queens — Astoria**: Greek, Middle Eastern, Eastern European

### Cuisine Strengths
NYC has everything, but is particularly dominant in: Pizza (all styles), Japanese (omakase, ramen), Chinese (regional), Italian, Korean, Indian, French, Jewish deli, Dominican, Mexican, Ethiopian, West African. NYC sets national trends.

### Local Notes
- Include outer borough restaurants — creators want hidden gems, not just Manhattan
- NYC is the most competitive restaurant market in the world
- Food trucks and food halls are significant (Smorgasburg, Chelsea Market, etc.)
- Price range is extreme — $1 pizza slices to $500 omakase

## 6. Atlanta, GA (Target: 60 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Buckhead**: Upscale, fine dining, steakhouses
- **Midtown**: Modern, diverse, growing restaurant scene
- **Old Fourth Ward / Ponce City Market**: Trendy, food hall, chef-driven
- **Decatur**: Independent, diverse, foodie destination
- **Inman Park / Little Five Points**: Eclectic, neighborhood spots
- **West Midtown / Westside**: Industrial-chic, breweries, modern restaurants
- **Buford Highway (BuHi)**: The most important immigrant food corridor in the Southeast — Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, Mexican, Salvadoran
- **East Atlanta Village**: Indie, diverse, up-and-coming
- **Summerhill**: New development, modern dining

### Cuisine Strengths
Atlanta excels in: Soul food, Southern, BBQ, Korean (BuHi), Chinese (BuHi), Vietnamese (BuHi), Jamaican/Caribbean, Ethiopian, Mexican, New American. Atlanta's Black food culture is nationally significant.

### Local Notes
- Buford Highway is a MUST for authentic international food
- Atlanta is a major food city that's nationally underrated
- Strong Black-owned restaurant scene
- Ponce City Market is a major creator content destination
- BBQ is serious here — include multiple styles

## 7. Chicago, IL (Target: 60 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **West Loop / Fulton Market**: Chicago's Restaurant Row — the most concentrated fine dining in the city
- **Lincoln Park**: Classic, family-friendly, diverse
- **Wicker Park / Bucktown**: Trendy, creative, cocktail-forward
- **Logan Square**: Chef-driven, hip, excellent cocktail scene
- **Pilsen**: Mexican, Latino culture, murals, emerging chef scene
- **Chinatown**: Authentic Chinese, dim sum, Sichuan
- **Andersonville**: Swedish roots, now diverse (Middle Eastern, Ethiopian, Japanese)
- **River North**: Nightlife-adjacent dining, steakhouses, celebrity chefs
- **Hyde Park**: University of Chicago area, diverse, affordable
- **Bridgeport**: Working-class roots, Chinese, Mexican, neighborhood spots

### Cuisine Strengths
Chicago is king of: Deep dish pizza, Italian beef, hot dogs, steakhouses. Also excellent: Mexican (Pilsen, Little Village), Chinese (Chinatown), Polish, Indian (Devon Ave), Korean, Japanese, Ethiopian, New American, molecular gastronomy (Alinea legacy).

### Local Notes
- Chicago's food identity is strong — include iconic Chicago-style foods
- West Loop / Fulton Market is where most of the national attention goes
- Pilsen is having a chef-driven renaissance while keeping its Mexican roots
- Strong neighborhood dining culture — Chicagoans eat in their neighborhoods

## 8. Los Angeles, CA (Target: 75 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Silver Lake / Echo Park**: Trendy, creative, modern California cuisine
- **Koreatown (K-Town)**: Korean BBQ capital, also Thai, Oaxacan, diverse
- **Little Tokyo / Arts District**: Japanese, ramen, creative dining, galleries
- **Hollywood / East Hollywood**: Thai (Thai Town), Armenian, diverse nightlife dining
- **West Hollywood (WeHo)**: Upscale, celebrity-frequented, health-conscious
- **Santa Monica**: Beach-adjacent, seafood, California casual
- **Venice / Abbot Kinney**: Trendy, brunch, health-conscious, artisan
- **Downtown LA (DTLA)**: Grand Central Market, revitalized dining scene
- **SGV (San Gabriel Valley)**: Chinese food capital of the US — dim sum, Sichuan, Taiwanese, Cantonese
- **Sawtelle**: Japanese, ramen corridor
- **Highland Park**: Emerging, diverse, affordable
- **Culver City**: Growing foodie scene, upscale casual

### Cuisine Strengths
LA is the king of: Mexican (all regional styles), Korean, Thai, Chinese (SGV), Japanese, Californian, health-conscious/vegan, Armenian, Persian, Salvadoran, Filipino. LA's food truck culture is legendary.

### Local Notes
- LA's dining is the most geographically spread out — neighborhoods matter enormously
- The SGV is the best Chinese food region in the Western hemisphere
- Taco culture is sacred — include both street tacos and upscale Mexican
- Health-conscious and vegan dining is more prominent here than anywhere
- Brunch is a lifestyle, not just a meal

## 9. Miami, FL (Target: 60 restaurants)

### Key Dining Neighborhoods
- **Wynwood**: Arts district, trendy, creative dining, cocktail bars
- **Brickell**: Financial district, upscale, international
- **Little Havana**: Cuban, Colombian, Central American — the soul of Miami
- **South Beach**: Celebrity dining, nightlife-adjacent, tourist-heavy but some gems
- **Design District**: Ultra-upscale, fashion-forward dining
- **Coconut Grove**: Neighborhood dining, waterfront, family-friendly
- **Coral Gables**: Upscale, Miracle Mile dining, Mediterranean
- **Little Haiti**: Haitian, Caribbean, emerging creative scene
- **Edgewater / Midtown**: Growing dining scene, modern concepts
- **Upper Buena Vista**: Hidden gem neighborhood, indie restaurants

### Cuisine Strengths
Miami is uniquely strong in: Cuban, Colombian, Peruvian (ceviche!), Venezuelan, Haitian, Jamaican, Brazilian, Argentinian, Japanese-Latin fusion (Nikkei), seafood, and high-end international. Miami's Latin American food diversity is unmatched in the US.

### Local Notes
- Latin American cuisine dominance — this is the most important thing about Miami food
- Ceviche and raw seafood culture is huge
- Celebrity chef outposts (from NYC, South America, Europe) are a big part of the scene
- Late-night dining culture — restaurants stay open later than most US cities
- Waterfront/outdoor dining year-round due to climate
