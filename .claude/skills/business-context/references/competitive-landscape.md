# Spot Competitive Landscape — Deep Reference

## Market Position

Spot occupies a unique space: **creator-first attribution for restaurant partnerships.** No existing player owns this intersection.

## Direct Competitors

### Mustard (mustard.love)
- **Model:** Restaurant-pays-creator marketplace
- **Pricing:** $299+/mo (restaurant side)
- **How it works:** Restaurant posts a brief, creators apply, restaurant picks and pays
- **Strengths:** Established marketplace, restaurant UX, existing deal flow
- **Weakness:** Restaurant-centric (creator has no control), no POS attribution, expensive for restaurants
- **Spot's advantage:** Creator-first model, 6x cheaper entry point, closed-loop POS attribution proves real ROI

### Beli (beli.com)
- **Model:** Consumer restaurant discovery app
- **Pricing:** Free (consumer-facing)
- **How it works:** Users rate and discover restaurants, social features
- **Strengths:** Clean consumer UX, growing user base, social discovery
- **Weakness:** No creator tools, no attribution, no B2B revenue model
- **Spot's advantage:** Creator-attribution layer that Beli lacks entirely; B2B revenue model

### Yelp
- **Model:** Consumer reviews + advertising platform
- **Pricing:** Ad-based (restaurant pays for placement)
- **Strengths:** Massive user base, brand recognition, SEO dominance
- **Weakness:** Legacy pay-to-play model, no creator integration, declining trust
- **Spot's advantage:** Tracks actual foot traffic and POS data, not just reviews

## Indirect Competitors

| Solution | What Creators Use It For | Why Spot Wins |
|----------|-------------------------|---------------|
| Spreadsheets + DMs | Managing partnerships manually | Professional SaaS tooling with tracking |
| Linktree / Stan Store | Creator landing pages | No restaurant-specific features or attribution |
| Later / Hootsuite | Social scheduling | No attribution tracking back to restaurant visits |
| Generic CRMs | Client management | Not built for creator-restaurant dynamics |

## dcspot.com Relationship

dcspot.com is a WordPress brochure site that serves as the DC Spot brand's marketing front door:

- **Domain authority:** dcspot.com has existing SEO/domain authority in DC food space
- **Role:** Marketing and discovery portal — drives awareness
- **Spot Platform role:** Operational backend — where the actual product lives
- **Recommendation:** Keep dcspot.com for brand awareness and SEO. Spot Platform handles all operational features.

The two are complementary, not competing.

## Spot's Defensible Moats

1. **Closed-loop POS attribution** — No competitor connects creator content → QR scan → POS transaction. This is technically hard to replicate (requires POS partnerships, OAuth flows, data matching).
2. **Creator-first model** — Creator controls the relationship. This aligns incentives correctly and is hard for restaurant-first platforms to reverse.
3. **Data network effects** — More creators using Spot = more attribution data = better benchmarks = more valuable for every creator on the platform.
4. **Low price point** — $49–$149/mo vs Mustard's $299+ makes Spot accessible to emerging creators, capturing the larger bottom of the funnel.
5. **Multi-POS integration** — Square (live), Clover (live), Toast (pending). Each integration is a competitive moat that takes months to build.

## Positioning Rules for Code/Content

When writing copy, building features, or making product decisions:
- Never position Spot as "restaurants paying creators to post" — that's the old model we're disrupting
- Always lead with attribution and proof of value
- Emphasize creator control and professional tools
- Restaurant value prop is "measurable ROI on creator partnerships" not "hire influencers"
- Price comparisons should reference Mustard ($299+) to show Spot's accessibility
