# Content Creator Skill — The Food Influencer Perspective

This skill encodes the mindset, motivations, and decision-making framework of food content creators on social media. Spot Platform is a creator-driven attribution platform, and if creators don't find value here, the platform fails. Every feature that touches the creator experience should be informed by this perspective.

## The Spot Philosophy — Nobody Gets Exploited

Spot is NOT a pay-to-play platform. The core belief is:

- **Restaurants should NOT be paying influencers to come eat and post.** A struggling restaurant shouldn't have to shell out $500-$2,000 to get a creator to walk through their door. That's exploitative and backwards.
- **Creators create content because that's what they do.** They eat at restaurants, they film, they post. That's their craft. They get paid by their platform (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube) based on views and engagement.
- **Spot is the attribution layer.** When a creator posts about a restaurant and new customers show up, Spot tracks that value. The creator sets up QR codes, deals, and trackable links. When those drive traffic, everybody profits — the restaurant gets customers, the creator gets attribution credit, and real value is measured.
- **The mutual benefit is the deal.** Restaurants offer deals/perks through the creator's Spot link. Creators get content + attribution + a percentage of trackable value generated. Nobody pays out of pocket for something that hasn't generated results.

This philosophy shapes everything. When in doubt, ask: "Is this making someone pay for something that hasn't proven value yet?" If yes, redesign it.

## Who Is the Spot Creator?

Spot targets **food content creators** at all levels — people who create content about restaurants, food culture, and dining experiences. They range from someone with 2K followers doing it for passion to someone with 500K making a living from it.

### Creator Tiers

**Emerging (2K–25K followers)**
- Building their audience and developing their voice
- Post about restaurants they genuinely love
- Don't have brand deals yet — Spot is their first real tool for monetizing their passion
- Most excited about tracking the real impact of their content
- Will use Spot because it gives them something to offer restaurants beyond "I'll post about you"

**Established (25K–100K followers)**
- Have a recognizable style and consistent posting schedule
- Know their engagement rates and can show real audience impact
- Already get some free meals and occasional paid gigs
- Use Spot to professionalize their restaurant relationships with real data
- Core Spot user — wants to prove their value in dollars, not just likes

**Professional (100K–500K followers)**
- Content creation is their primary income
- Very selective about which restaurants they feature — brand alignment matters
- Use Spot to manage ongoing relationships and track attribution at scale
- May negotiate premium deals where restaurants offer exclusive access/experiences

## What Creators Actually Want

### 1. Proof That Their Content Drives Real Business
The biggest frustration for food creators is that they KNOW their content sends people to restaurants, but they can't prove it. Spot fixes this. When a creator can show "my post about your brunch sent 47 people through your door last Saturday," that changes everything. It transforms them from "someone who posts food pics" to "a real marketing channel with measurable ROI."

### 2. Creative Freedom
Creators care deeply about making content that feels authentic. The best content comes from genuine experiences — a creator who actually loves a dish will make better content about it than one who's being paid to pretend. Spot campaigns should define what's interesting about the restaurant (the story, the dish, the vibe) and let the creator tell it their way.

### 3. A Fair Way to Earn
Creators earn through multiple channels on Spot:
- **Platform revenue** — Views on TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts generate direct income from those platforms
- **Attribution bonuses** — When their QR codes/deals drive trackable traffic to a restaurant, they earn a cut of that value
- **Exclusive access** — Complimentary dining, kitchen access, event invites — not as "payment" but as tools to create better content
- **Deal commissions** — When a follower redeems a deal the creator shared, the creator earns attribution credit

The key principle: **the creator earns when they generate value, not by extracting money from restaurants.**

### 4. Content That Performs
Creators need their content to get views, likes, and shares. This means:
- The experience needs to be inherently interesting — unique dishes, beautiful plating, behind-the-scenes access, chef stories
- Timing matters — restaurant openings, seasonal menus, food trend moments get more traction
- The restaurant's vibe needs to photograph/film well — lighting, ambiance, plating all matter
- The creator needs freedom to frame the story with whatever hook will work for their audience

### 5. Tools, Not Gatekeepers
Creators want Spot to be a tool that empowers them, not a middleman that controls access. They want to:
- Discover interesting restaurants in their city
- Set up their own deals and QR codes
- Track how many people they've sent to a restaurant
- Build a portfolio of proven attribution results
- Reach out to restaurants directly with data backing them up

## Content Formats — What Creators Actually Produce

### Short-Form Video (highest impact)
- **Instagram Reel** (15-90 seconds) — the workhorse format. Algorithm-favored, high reach
- **TikTok** (15-180 seconds) — younger audience, trend-driven, highest potential virality
- **YouTube Short** (up to 60 seconds) — growing format, good for reach

### Static Posts
- **Instagram Carousel** (up to 20 images) — great for "food tour" style content, high save rate
- **Instagram Single Photo** — declining but still relevant for aesthetic feeds

### Stories (ephemeral, 24hr)
- **Instagram Stories** (set of 3-8 frames) — casual, behind-the-scenes feel. Great for real-time visits and sharing deal codes/QR links

### Long-Form
- **YouTube Video** (5-15 minutes) — highest production value, in-depth reviews
- **Blog Post** — SEO value, detailed reviews

### Creator's Choice
Many creators prefer to decide the format after visiting, because the experience itself determines what content will work best. A photogenic dish might inspire a carousel; an energetic kitchen calls for a reel; a compelling chef story becomes a longer video. Smart campaigns let creators choose.

## Campaign Types — From the Creator's Perspective

On Spot, a "campaign" isn't "restaurant pays creator to post." A campaign is the creator organizing their content creation around a restaurant with attribution tracking. Here's how they think about it:

### Visit & Create
The most common. Creator visits a restaurant, creates content, and tracks attribution.
- **What the creator does:** Visits on their schedule, creates whatever content the experience inspires
- **What the restaurant offers:** A deal for the creator's audience (10% off, free appetizer, etc.)
- **How everyone profits:** Creator gets content + platform revenue + attribution credit when followers redeem the deal. Restaurant gets new customers they can measure.

### Menu Feature
Creator highlights specific dishes or a new menu. More focused than a general visit.
- **What the creator does:** Coordinates with restaurant on what's new/notable, creates focused content
- **What the restaurant offers:** Complimentary tasting + a deal for the creator's audience
- **How everyone profits:** Creator gets premium content (new/exclusive items perform well). Restaurant gets buzz around specific offerings.

### Event Coverage
Creator covers a restaurant event — opening night, tasting, chef's dinner.
- **What the creator does:** Attends event, creates real-time stories + polished follow-up content
- **What the restaurant offers:** Event access + audience deal
- **How everyone profits:** Creator gets exclusive content (events = high engagement). Restaurant gets amplified event reach + trackable post-event traffic.

### Behind the Scenes
Creator gets kitchen access, chef time, sourcing stories. Premium content that audiences love.
- **What the creator does:** Extended time at restaurant, films in kitchen, interviews chef/staff
- **What the restaurant offers:** Full access + chef availability + audience deal
- **How everyone profits:** Creator gets deep, story-driven content (highest save/share rates). Restaurant gets authentic brand storytelling.

### Ongoing Relationship
Creator becomes a regular voice for a restaurant over time. Most valuable for both sides.
- **What the creator does:** Visits regularly, creates content consistently, becomes a genuine ambassador
- **What the restaurant offers:** Standing comp + exclusive access + premium audience deals
- **How everyone profits:** Creator builds ongoing content series (algorithm loves consistency). Restaurant gets a sustained, trusted marketing channel with proven attribution.

## The Value Exchange — How It Actually Works

The traditional influencer model is broken: restaurants pay → creator posts → maybe something happens. Spot flips this:

1. **Creator discovers a restaurant** on Spot (or adds one they already love)
2. **Creator creates a campaign** — chooses the type, sets up a deal/QR code for their audience
3. **Creator visits, creates content, posts it** — with their Spot tracking link or deal code embedded
4. **Followers see the content, visit the restaurant, redeem the deal** — Spot tracks every conversion
5. **Everyone sees the results** — creator builds their attribution portfolio, restaurant sees real customer data, Spot captures the connection

The money flows from **results, not promises:**
- Creators earn from platform ad revenue (views) + attribution commissions (deal redemptions)
- Restaurants invest only in deals that are redeemed (cost of the discount), not in upfront payments to creators
- Some premium arrangements exist (standing comp, exclusive access) but these are earned through proven track record, not demanded upfront

## What Makes a Creator Say Yes to a Spot Campaign

1. **"Is this restaurant worth my content?"** — Does it match my brand? Will my audience care? Is the food/vibe interesting enough to make great content?
2. **"Can I track the value I create?"** — Does Spot give me attribution data I can build my portfolio with?
3. **"Is the deal good for my audience?"** — Will my followers actually want this? A bad deal reflects poorly on the creator.
4. **"Do I have creative freedom?"** — Can I tell the story my way?
5. **"Is there a path to an ongoing relationship?"** — One-off visits are fine, but the real value is becoming a restaurant's go-to creator.

## What Makes a Creator Say No

1. **"They want me to pay to feature them"** — Never. The creator is the one generating value.
2. **"The deal they're offering my audience is weak"** — "5% off on Tuesdays" isn't worth a creator's credibility.
3. **"They want to approve my content before I post"** — Kills authenticity. Audience can tell.
4. **"There's nothing interesting to create about"** — Generic food, bad lighting, nothing story-worthy.
5. **"No way to track if my content actually did anything"** — Without attribution, it's just content in the void.

## Applying This to Spot Platform Features

When building any creator-facing feature, ask:

- **Does this empower the creator or extract from the restaurant?** — Spot should create value, not transfer it
- **Does this give the creator attribution data?** — If they can't prove their impact, the feature is incomplete
- **Does this help the creator's audience?** — Good deals for followers = good content = everyone wins
- **Does this respect creative freedom?** — Describe what's interesting, don't script the content
- **Does this build toward ongoing relationships?** — One-offs are fine, but the platform should incentivize sustained partnerships
